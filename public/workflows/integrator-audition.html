The PR Hub · The Integrator Audition

Could this hire become your Integrator?

Maybe. But test into it — never drift into it. This is the dual-track audition for a senior hire who might grow into your right hand: prove they can run an account front-to-back, and probe — separately — whether they can run the firm. Two different bets. Win the first before you place the second.

Companion to Hiring Senior Leads & the Integrator spec.

The trap to avoid

"She's brilliant at delivery" must never quietly become "she'll make a great Integrator."

Running an account is craft. Running the firm is operations. Most brilliant executors have one muscle, not both — which is exactly why they end up back inside agencies rather than building their own. Test the two muscles separately, in order.

Two tracks, run in parallel

Track 1 · prove first

Execution

Can she run an account front-to-back, to my standard?

  • The five hiring tasks (90-day plan, newsjack, red pen, AI-leverage, hard client moment)
  • Then real accounts: is she green on her own book?
  • The quality bar is yours until she's earned it
The gate: if she can't clear this, the Integrator question is moot. You can't have someone running delivery who can't do delivery.
Track 2 · probe alongside

The operating muscle

Can she run the firm — people, process, numbers, accountability?

  • Watch the five signals below in everyday work
  • Give her a small slice of ops to run and see what happens
  • Different signals entirely from craft — don't conflate them
The gate: "can't build a business" is fine (that's your job). "Can't run one operationally" is not. That's what you're testing.

The five Integrator signals

You won't find these in the execution tasks — you find them in how she works over weeks. Watch for them deliberately.

1 · Systems instinct

Does she turn "how we did this" into a repeatable process — or does it all live in her head?

Test it: ask her to document how she onboarded a client. Great = a reusable checklist others could follow.

2 · Financial comfort

Can she read a P&L and a capacity plan without flinching? Does she think in margin and utilisation?

Test it: walk her through the model. Great = she asks sharp questions about margin and load, not glazed silence.

3 · Accountability spine

Will she hold another senior person to a deadline — and have the hard conversation? This is the one most executors fail.

Test it: give her a peer coordination task. Great = she chases, sets expectations, and doesn't just do it herself to avoid friction.

4 · Team temperament

Does she light up making others excellent — or does she need to be the one doing the great work herself?

Test it: have her brief and review the associate's output. Great = she coaches and elevates, and takes pride in their win.

5 · Runs the rhythm

Does she run a tight, on-time meeting that ends in decisions — or let it sag into a chat?

Test it: hand her the weekly team check-in for a month. Great = agenda, scorecard first, issues closed, on time.

Where she lands — the decision

Score the two tracks independently, then read the quadrant. Every outcome except one is a good outcome — which is exactly why the audition de-risks the bet.

How to structure it

A designed audition, not a promise. She joins as a Senior Lead — that's the title and the deal today — with the Integrator path named openly as something to be earned.

Say this to her — plainly
"I want to see you run accounts brilliantly first. If that's there, and you show me you can run people and process, there's a real path to you running the day-to-day of the firm with me. Earned, not given — and we'll both know within six months."
DAY 1

Set the frame

  • Title: Senior Account Lead
  • Name the Integrator path honestly
  • Give her the model, tiers & bench
  • Agree what you're both testing for
90 DAYS

Execution gate

  • Is she green on her own accounts?
  • Clients happy? Quality at standard?
  • Any early Integrator signals showing?
  • If execution isn't there — she's a lead, not more
~6 MONTHS

Integrator gate

  • Is she running others well?
  • Documenting process, holding the line?
  • Comfortable with the numbers & rhythm?
  • If yes — put "Integrator" on the table
On the history & the loyalty

Nine years of wanting to work with you is real — it means trust, shared values, and someone who'll walk through walls for you. That's gold in a right hand. But it's also the thing that will make you generous when you should be objective.

Decide on evidence, not affection. The kindest thing you can do for a friendship is not put a friend in a role they'll fail in. And name the real risk out loud: if you make her Integrator on hope and she turns out to be a craftsperson, not an operator, you lose twice — the function and the friend. The audition is exactly what prevents that.

— Chris Savage, advisory lens
