The Senior-Led Agency

The old agency sold you a senior team and gave you a junior one. We do the opposite. Every client is run by someone who has done this for twelve years — and their bench is not a room of graduates. It is artificial intelligence they command like a newsroom.

This is how we are built, how we work, and why it wins.

Operating model · v1 · Owned by the Founder & CEO · Reviewed quarterly

The one idea

We don't sell more. We sell better — and we've engineered the cost of "better" down to where nobody else can follow.

Every decision in this document ladders back to that sentence. If a hire, a process, or a service doesn't make the work better or the delivery of it cheaper to produce at the same quality, we don't do it. Volume is a race to the bottom run by agencies who confuse activity with value. We are not in that race.

Four laws we don't break

Break one and the model stops working. They are not aspirations. They are constraints.

LAW 01

A senior in every room

The person who wins the client is the person who runs the client. No pitch-and-switch. Ever. Founders of eight- and nine-figure businesses want adults, not apprentices.

LAW 02

AI is the junior bench

We don't hire graduates to draft. We built prompts, refined over years, that write the first draft, the award entry, the long-form. The senior edits to exceptional. That's the whole trick.

LAW 03

Quality over quantity

We deliver against the client's north star, not a content quota. Fewer, sharper moves that a CEO actually notices beat a monthly report full of noise.

LAW 04

Smarter, not harder

Leverage is the point. If our people are working longer hours, we've built the model wrong. Time saved is reinvested into thinking — the one thing clients can't get anywhere else.

How we're built

Flat on purpose. Four layers, no fiefdoms, one P&L. Borrowed from the best law-firm-style partnerships in strategic communications — not the pyramid agencies we came from.

Who owns what

Layer 1

Founder & CEO

The face, the rainmaker, the final word on counsel.

Accountable for

  • The personal brand engine — workshops, webinars, podcasts, interviews, collateral.
  • New business: sourcing, securing, and signing. The pipeline is the founder's number.
  • Weekly accountability meeting with every lead — structured, on the EOS rhythm.
  • Renewals strategy, jointly owned with the lead. Nothing lapses by surprise.
  • Guarding the model. The four laws are the founder's to defend.
Layer 2 · optional

BD / Lead Manager

Added the moment the founder becomes the bottleneck — not a day later.

Accountable for

  • Running inbound leads and calls the founder generates through to onboarding and sign-off.
  • Freeing the founder to do the two things only the founder can: brand and close.
Layer 3 · the engine

Senior Strategic Account Leads

10–12+ years. They bring the strategy to the founder, not the other way round.

Accountable for

  • Owning the client end-to-end: discovery, the 90-day plan, and the counsel in the room.
  • Every signed deliverable in the contract — knows it cold, delivers it, reports it.
  • Commanding the AI bench: brief it, review it, edit it to exceptional. The lead is the editor, never the typist.
  • Reading the room and the news — AFR, The Australian — spotting the angle before the client does.
  • Renewal radar: knows every contract date and flags it internally well ahead of time.
  • Working with the admin assistant to keep workflows, the coverage database, and reporting current.
Layer 3.5 · the bench

The AI Junior Bench

Not a person. A capability. The best copywriter and researcher you never have to manage.

Deployed for

  • First-draft long-form, media pitches, LinkedIn content, and award submissions.
  • Desk research, media list building, angle generation, and first-pass monitoring.
  • Reporting scaffolds — the numbers assembled, the senior adds the meaning.
Layer 4

Administrative Assistant

The operational spine. Makes the seniors faster by owning everything that isn't counsel.

Accountable for

  • Media monitoring and coverage capture into the central database (links, always current).
  • Reporting production support — weekly, quarterly, annual.
  • Back-end filing. The systems are sophisticated; clients have access at all times.
  • Workflow hygiene across every account.

The bench, governed

This is the part that makes or breaks us — and the part most likely to embarrass an agency that does it lazily. A PR firm that lets AI hallucinate a fact into a media pitch has burned the one asset it sells: trust. So the bench runs on gates, and a senior signs every one.

Brief

The lead briefs the bench with a governed prompt from the TPH library — client context, angle, tone, the non-negotiables. Rubbish in, rubbish out; the brief is where quality is decided.

Owner: Senior lead

Draft

The bench produces the first draft — long-form, pitch, entry, or research. Fast, cheap, and never client-facing in this state.

Owner: AI bench · TPH prompt library

Fact & source check

Every claim verified against a real source. This gate is mandatory and it is human. No exceptions for speed.

Owner: Senior lead (+ admin support)

Edit to exceptional

The lead does what the lead is paid for: judgment, nuance, the sentence a CEO remembers. Draft becomes counsel.

Owner: Senior lead

Ship & log

Out to the client or the journalist. Coverage and assets logged to the central database. The loop closes.

Owner: Senior lead + admin
Strategic counsel — protect the bench

The prompt library is now a company asset worth more than any single employee. Treat it like one: version it, control who can change it, and log which prompt produced which client work. When a lead leaves, the leverage stays.

Assign one owner for the prompt library and review it quarterly. Undocumented prompts are a risk, not an asset.

Strategic counsel — one boat, one bonus

Brunswick's edge isn't its people — plenty of firms have good people. It's the structure: one shared P&L, one bonus pool, no individual profit centres. Nobody hoards a client. In a flat firm of senior egos, that is the difference between collaboration and quiet war.

Add a small shared-firm component on top of the individual retention bonus, so a lead helping another lead's client is rewarded, not resented.

Where I'd push back

You asked for counsel, not applause. The model is strong — genuinely. Here are the three places it will be tested, and what I'd do about each before it costs you.

1 · Six accounts per lead is a ceiling you earn, not a floor you assume

Elite senior-led firms run 4–5 high-touch strategic accounts per person. The pyramid agencies run 6–8 — but only because a junior army absorbs the load. Your bet is that the AI bench closes that gap so a senior can carry six alone. It's a good bet. It is not yet a proven one.

Start leads at 4–5. Ramp to 6 only once the bench is demonstrably doing junior-grade work at senior-grade quality. And weight by complexity: a crisis or activism-style account should count as two.

2 · "No juniors" solves cost and creates a succession hole

If you never hire junior, you have no bench of future senior leads, and every senior who leaves takes a client relationship with them. The best firms are quietly becoming the primary developers of senior talent, because that's now the scarce thing.

Don't reintroduce the pyramid. But consider one apprentice per 2–3 leads, purely for succession — or an equity/partner path that makes your best leads owners, not flight risks.

3 · You are the single point of failure on new business

At six leads × six accounts, that's ~36 clients — and one person doing all the winning, all the closing, all the brand, and a weekly accountability meeting with every lead. The BD layer is drawn as "optional / later." At scale it isn't optional; it's the thing that stops you being the bottleneck.

Hire the BD / lead manager before the pain, not after. The trigger is when your own calendar starts costing you pipeline — and that comes sooner than it feels like it will.

Bonus · Price the value, not the hours

If your whole thesis is "better, not more," never sell time. The moment you quote hours you've told the client that hours are the value — and then AI making you faster becomes a reason to pay you less. Price the outcome against their north star.

Move to fixed quarterly scope priced on business impact, not effort. Let the leverage fall to your margin, where it belongs.
