The PR Hub · Hiring the Engine

Don't hire the résumé. Hire the room-holder.

You have people reaching out who want to lead the day-to-day. Good. Now find out — before you bet a client on them — whether they can actually do it. Twelve years on a CV means nothing until you've watched them think. This is what to test for, the tasks to set them, and how to score what comes back.

For the next 3–6 months you are their report line — so hire people you'd be proud to hand a founder-CEO to. Companion to the Integrator spec.

1 · What great actually looks like

Seven competencies. The first three are non-negotiable — no amount of the others makes up for a gap here. Score each candidate 1–5 (rubric in the companion tracker).

CompetencyWhat you're really testing
1 · Strategic thinking
non-negotiable		Can they build a 90-day plan from a business objective — not a list of tactics? Do they start with the client's north star, or with "let's do a press release"?
2 · Client gravitas
non-negotiable		Would a founder of an 8–9-figure business take their counsel seriously? Presence, judgment, the confidence to push back on a CEO.
3 · News & media instinct
non-negotiable		Do they read the AFR before the client does? Can they see the angle and the gap in today's news and place a client into it?
4 · Craft & editing eyeCan they take a draft to exceptional? Their job is editor, not typist — the standard lives in their red pen.
5 · AI leverageDo they see AI as their junior team, or fear it? Can they brief it well and critique its output ruthlessly?
6 · Ownership & autonomyWill they run six accounts without being managed? Do they bring you solutions, or problems?
7 · Commercial instinctDo they think about retention, renewal, and growing an account — or just servicing it?

2 · The tasks — set these, don't just interview

Talk is cheap and interviews reward the articulate. Give every serious candidate a paid short task (2–3 hours) plus a live session. You'll learn more in one afternoon of watching them work than in three conversations.

1

The 90-day plan

Take-home · ~2 hrs
The brief you give them
"Here's a fictional client: a A$40M founder-led SaaS business, the CEO wants to be seen as the category leader in [industry] within 12 months. Budget is a Momentum retainer. Give me your 90-day plan for their first quarter with us — outcomes first, then the activity that gets there."
Testing
  • Strategic thinking
  • Structure & prioritisation
  • Commercial judgment
Great looks like
  • Starts with the CEO's north star & audience
  • Chooses a few sharp moves, not a laundry list
  • Ties every activity to an outcome
  • Names what they'd measure
2

The newsjack

Live · 30 min
The brief you give them
"Here's today's AFR / The Australian. Take 20 minutes. Find me two stories our fictional SaaS CEO could credibly insert themselves into this week — and tell me the exact angle and who you'd pitch."
Testing
  • News & media instinct
  • Speed & relevance
  • Real media knowledge
Great looks like
  • Spots a non-obvious, credible angle
  • Knows which journalist / outlet
  • Protects the client from a bad take
  • Moves fast without going generic
3

The red pen

Live · 20 min
The brief you give them
"Here's a first-draft media pitch [give them a deliberately mediocre AI-written draft]. Mark it up as if it's going to a top-tier journalist tomorrow. Talk me through what you'd change and why."
Testing
  • Craft & editing eye
  • The quality bar
  • Can they lift, not just tidy
Great looks like
  • Cuts hard; sharpens the hook
  • Catches a weak or false claim
  • Raises it to a standard a CEO would be proud of
  • Has a clear, articulable standard
4

The AI-leverage test

Live · 20 min
The brief you give them
"Use AI to draft an award-entry opening for this client, then critique what it gave you. Show me how you'd brief it and where you'd never trust it."
Testing
  • AI leverage mindset
  • Judgment over output
  • Fit with our model
Great looks like
  • Briefs it with context & a clear ask
  • Treats output as a first draft, not gospel
  • Fact-checks instinctively
  • Excited by the leverage, not threatened
5

The hard client moment

Live role-play · 15 min
The brief you give them
"I'm your client CEO. I'm unhappy — 'we've had no coverage in six weeks and I'm questioning the value.' You have five minutes. Go."
Testing
  • Client gravitas
  • Composure under pressure
  • Ownership vs excuses
Great looks like
  • Calm, no defensiveness
  • Reframes to outcomes & the plan
  • Owns it, then leads
  • You'd trust them in the room alone
Pay for the task

Pay senior candidates for the take-home. It signals you value their time, it filters out the unserious, and it earns you the right to judge real work. A few hundred dollars is the cheapest hiring insurance you'll ever buy.

— Chris Savage, advisory lens

3 · Green flags & red flags

Green flags

  • Brings you a point of view, not just questions
  • Talks in outcomes and business impact
  • Real, current media relationships they can name
  • Reads widely — quotes today's news unprompted
  • Curious and fluent about AI as a tool
  • Has run senior accounts solo before
  • Asks about the client, not just the perks

Red flags

  • Tactics-first: "I'd do a press release"
  • Name-drops titles but can't name journalists
  • Needs the plan handed to them
  • Defensive when challenged
  • Threatened by or dismissive of AI
  • Only ever worked inside a big-agency pyramid with juniors doing the work
  • Talks about hours, not results

4 · Interview questions that reveal the truth

Behavioural, not hypothetical. "Tell me about a time…" beats "What would you do if…" every time.

  1. "Walk me through a campaign you personally led end-to-end. What was the business result — not the coverage, the result?"
  2. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a CEO client. What did you do?"
  3. "What did you read this week, and what client opportunity did it spark?"
  4. "Show me a piece of writing you're proud of, and one you'd now rewrite. What changed?"
  5. "How are you using AI in your work today? Where do you not trust it?"
  6. "You'll carry up to six accounts here with no junior team, just AI and an associate. How does that land with you?"
  7. "A client is up for renewal and gone quiet. What have you already done about it?"

5 · Managing them yourself — the next 3–6 months

You're their report line until the Integrator lands and the processes bed in. That's fine — it's actually how you'll learn what to hand the Integrator. Keep it light, structured, and standard-setting.

  1. Weekly 1:1 (30 min each). Their accounts' health (R/A/G), what they need from you, what's at risk. Structured, not a chat.
  2. The quality gate is you — at first. Review their first few client-facing pieces personally, so the standard is set. Then let go.
  3. Document as you go. Every "here's how we do it" becomes a process note. That library is what you hand the Integrator on day one.
  4. Onboard against the model. Give them the operating model, the tiers, and the bench prompts in week one. Clarity up front prevents drift.
  5. 90-day embed check. Are their accounts green? Are clients happy? Are they autonomous yet? If yes, you've hired well and freed real time.
A word before you say yes to the eager ones

People wanting to "do the execution and lead the day-to-day" is a wonderful problem — but enthusiasm isn't evidence. Set the task. Watch them think. The ones who light up at a hard brief and bring you something sharp are your firm's future. The ones who just talk a good game will cost you a client. Hire slow here; it's the most important bet you'll make this year.

— Chris Savage, advisory lens
